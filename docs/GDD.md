# Living World RPG — Game Design Document

**Working Title.** A living-world fantasy RPG where ancient gods, magical
technology, giant mechs, and spaceships coexist.

Art Direction • World • Systems • Scope

Source deck: [`Living_World_RPG_GDD.pptx`](./Living_World_RPG_GDD.pptx)

---

## 1. Game Vision

The experience we are trying to create.

- **Core idea** — A solo RPG set in a living world that continues to change without the player.
- **Player role** — The player is not the center of the universe; they are one person capable of interfering with other people's lives.
- **Fantasy** — Ancient fantasy societies are inheriting the technology of retired gods and interpreting it as magic.
- **Scale** — Start personal and local, then gradually reveal kingdoms, worlds, spaceships, mechs, and the gods themselves.
- **Goal** — Make players feel that they are living through a period of history rather than completing a list of quests.

## 2. Design Pillars

- **Living World** — NPCs have routines, relationships, goals, and autonomy.
- **Global Time** — A persistent calendar drives recurring and scheduled events.
- **Player Intervention** — Players can enter people's lives and redirect outcomes.
- **Ancient Mystery** — Godly technology survives as artifacts, ruins, magic, mechs, and ships.
- **Personal → Cosmic** — Small choices can eventually connect to larger historical events.

## 3. World Concept

Ancient fantasy entering an industrial and spacefaring age.

- **The Gods** — The gods did not die. They retired and became observers, leaving their civilizations behind.
- **Godly Civilization** — Ancient infrastructure, weapons, machines, and knowledge survive long after their creators withdrew.
- **Modern Interpretation** — Mortals call many remnants magical artifacts because the original technology is poorly understood.
- **Industrialization** — Magic and recovered technology are being adapted into factories, railways, airships, and modern industry.
- **Spacefaring Age** — The same civilization eventually reaches orbit and begins interacting with other worlds.

## 4. Worlds & Pantheons

Each world carries the cultural identity of its former gods.

- **World diversity** — Different worlds were governed by different pantheons, producing distinct cultures, architecture, religions, technology, and magic.
- **Example: Shinto-inspired world** — Shrines, spirits, sacred forests, ancestral traditions, and spirit-based magic.
- **Example: Greco-Roman-inspired world** — Marble cities, temples, heroic traditions, imperial infrastructure, and divine/celestial magic.
- **Megaworlds** — Some planets are small frontier worlds; others are enormous civilization-spanning city worlds.
- **Shared history** — Despite cultural differences, all worlds are connected by the ancient godly civilization and the same wider universe.

## 5. Magic & Technology

Different forms of magic are cultural interfaces with a deeper system.

- **Magic is real** — Modern people can genuinely manipulate supernatural forces, even if they misunderstand their origins.
- **Different traditions** — Spirit magic, rune magic, elemental magic, divine magic, astral magic, alchemy, and magitech can all exist.
- **Cultural interpretation** — The same underlying ancient principles may be expressed very differently from world to world.
- **Artifacts** — Ancient machines may be treated as holy relics, magical weapons, shrines, ruins, or mythical creatures.
- **Discovery** — The player gradually learns that magic and ancient technology are more closely related than modern cultures realize.

## 6. Art Direction

A deliberate mixed-media identity.

- **Characters** — Handcrafted pixel art inspired by *Last Ditch Effort*: expressive, frame-by-frame animation and strong silhouettes.
- **Backgrounds** — Hand-drawn, painterly 2D environments. The world itself should feel like an animated fantasy film.
- **Presentation** — Cinematic 2D camera, parallax, atmospheric depth, dramatic scale, and environmental animation.
- **Contrast** — Crisp pixel characters intentionally contrast with organic painted environments.
- **Technology** — Mechs and spacecraft should feel enormous, old, mechanical, and integrated into the natural world.
- **Target feeling** — A beautiful living fantasy world where the player can look up and see airships or spaceships crossing the sky.

## 7. Autonomous NPCs

NPCs have lives that exist independently of the player.

- **Each important NPC has** — Personality, needs, relationships, occupation, routines, goals, knowledge, and long-term plans.
- **Daily life** — NPCs work, eat, travel, socialize, sleep, celebrate, argue, form relationships, and pursue their own goals.
- **Autonomy** — NPCs can make progress or experience setbacks without the player being present.
- **Relationships** — Friendships, rivalries, romances, family ties, business partnerships, and grudges can change over time.
- **Visible simulation** — The player should be able to observe lives changing rather than only reading about them.

## 8. Global Calendar & Timeline

One shared calendar drives the world.

- **Persistent calendar** — The entire game world shares an in-game calendar with days, seasons, years, and recurring dates.
- **Recurring events** — Festivals, birthdays, memorials, harvests, religious ceremonies, competitions, and seasonal traditions.
- **Scheduled events** — Political meetings, train openings, discoveries, launches, wars, migrations, and other historical events.
- **Conditional outcomes** — An event can occur on a known date but resolve differently depending on world state.
- **Time moves without you** — If the player spends weeks elsewhere, the world does not wait. Events happen and NPCs continue living.

## 9. Player Intervention

The player creates routes by interfering with existing lives.

- **Observe** — Follow people, learn routines, listen to conversations, and discover what is happening.
- **Intervene** — Help, deceive, persuade, recruit, sabotage, protect, ignore, or otherwise alter an NPC's trajectory.
- **No traditional route menu** — The game does not present "Route A / Route B". Routes emerge from actions and timing.
- **Missed opportunities** — Events can happen without the player. Missing something is a valid outcome.
- **Persistent consequences** — A small decision can alter an NPC, a family, a business, a faction, or eventually a region.

## 10. Unintended & Dark Paths

The game should not label morality for the player.

- **No obvious morality meter** — The player should not see a simple GOOD / EVIL score.
- **Incomplete information** — Players act based on what they know, which may be incomplete or wrong.
- **Reasonable decisions can compound** — A helpful decision can create consequences that only become visible much later.
- **Dark path** — The player may gradually enable exploitation, political oppression, war, or other harmful outcomes without realizing the larger chain.
- **The world remembers** — Consequences are shown through changed people, places, relationships, politics, and history rather than morality notifications.

## 11. Mechs, Airships & Space

Cosmic scale exists inside the living world.

- **Airships** — Common enough to populate the sky and connect regions, but still visually impressive.
- **Spaceships** — Evidence that civilization is expanding beyond individual worlds. Early on, they can remain distant background elements.
- **Mechs** — Ancient and modern giant machines used for warfare, construction, exploration, religion, or other purposes.
- **Scale** — Technology should dwarf ordinary people. A mech can become a landmark; a ship can fill the sky.
- **Progression** — The player can gradually move from seeing these things, to interacting with them, to eventually traveling beyond their home world.

## 12. Core RPG Loop

The simulation supports the RPG instead of replacing it.

1. Explore
2. Meet people
3. Learn their lives
4. Choose whether to intervene
5. Fight / solve problems
6. Time advances
7. World changes
8. Discover consequences

Traditional RPG progression (levels, equipment, magic, combat) sits underneath this loop.

## 13. Solo-Dev Scope

Build a dense corner of the universe, not the whole universe.

- **World** — 1 main planet/region for the first game, with a second world used to demonstrate the wider universe.
- **Locations** — 1 major town or city, 2–3 smaller settlements, 1 wilderness region, 4–6 dungeons/major locations, 1 spaceport/orbital location.
- **Characters** — 4–6 playable characters and roughly 30–50 meaningful NPCs.
- **Content** — 8–12 major bosses, roughly 20–30 enemy archetypes, and a focused story.
- **Vehicles** — 1 important party ship/vehicle and a small number of major mech encounters or story machines.
- **Target length** — Approximately 15–25 hours for the first full game.

## 14. Development Strategy

Prototype the hardest idea first.

1. **NPC life** — Build 5 NPCs with schedules, goals, needs, and relationships. Let them live for 30 in-game days.
2. **Calendar** — Add fixed, conditional, and recurring events to the timeline.
3. **Intervention** — Allow the player to disrupt NPC plans and observe the consequences.
4. **Presentation** — Create one polished town using the hand-painted background + pixel character pipeline.
5. **RPG layer** — Add exploration, combat, equipment, magic, and progression.
6. **Vertical slice** — Combine village → wilderness → ancient machine → boss → major world event into a 30–60 minute playable slice.

## 15. Vertical Slice

The smallest build that proves the entire concept.

- **Location** — One beautiful town with 5–10 active NPCs.
- **World simulation** — NPC routines, relationships, and a 30-day calendar.
- **Event** — A scheduled local event that occurs whether or not the player participates.
- **Intervention** — The player can alter at least one NPC's planned trajectory.
- **Adventure** — A nearby wilderness area and one ancient godly ruin.
- **Spectacle** — An airship or spaceship crosses the sky; the player discovers a massive ancient machine.
- **Payoff** — The player returns to town and can visibly see that the world changed because of time and/or their actions.

## 16. What NOT to Build Yet

Protect the project from scope explosion.

- A fully explorable galaxy
- Dozens of planets
- Hundreds of fully simulated NPCs
- Large mech combat systems
- Complex spaceship combat
- Every pantheon and its complete mythology
- Multiple giant cities
- A massive branching main story
- Fully simulated economies across every world

These belong to the universe bible, not the first playable build.

## North Star

> Make the player feel like they are living in a world that would exist
> without them.
>
> The gods create the history. The world carries it forward. NPCs live
> inside it. The player changes it.

## Technical Direction

Engine, implementation philosophy, and maintainability.

- **Engine** — Unreal Engine 5.5.4.
- **Primary implementation** — C++ / traditional code for core gameplay and system architecture.
- **Blueprint philosophy** — Use Blueprints only when necessary or when Unreal's visual tools provide a clear practical advantage.
- **Build order** — Complete and stabilize core code systems first, then expose only the functionality that needs to be authored or adjusted through Blueprints.
- **Core systems in code** — NPC simulation, schedules, relationships, calendar, world state, event logic, progression, and other foundational systems should remain primarily code-driven.
- **Blueprint use cases** — Content setup, visual scripting where appropriate, Sequencer/cinematic work, exposed tuning, and other tasks where Blueprint materially improves iteration.
- **Goal** — Keep the project maintainable and scalable for a solo developer while still taking advantage of Unreal's content and visual tools.

**Technical target:** Unreal Engine 5.5.4
